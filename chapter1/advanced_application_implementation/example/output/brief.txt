Composition. Place three mugs as a tight triangular cluster in the lower-left to lower-center of a 4:5 portrait canvas, with the tallest mug as hero at the left third and the two shorter mugs stepping back and right, partially overlapping. Let the linen cloth run diagonally from lower left to mid-right, and reserve the upper-right third as quiet negative space for type and breathing room. Keep the horizon line implied by the cloth fold, not a hard table edge, and keep mugs, linen, and background on separate layers for relight.

Palette. Use #1B1815 as the deep shadow and background anchor, #6B5748 as the warm umber linen midtone, #B9A58C as the oat linen highlight, #3F5B58 as the muted teal glaze accent on one mug, and #E2D3B8 as the soft cream for rim light and typography. The teal should be the only cool note, small and deliberate, while the creams stay restrained to avoid a sweet or bright still life.

Light. Key from upper left, low and soft, raking across the rims and handles to reveal ceramic thickness and linen weave. Aim for high contrast with deep, warm falloff to the right, but keep the shadow side readable rather than crushed. Protect highlights by holding specular hits on glaze below roughly 90 percent luminance, preserving linen fiber detail in the brightest folds, and using a subtle negative fill on the right to prevent flatness.

Typography. Set in Cormorant Garamond Regular, with italic reserved for a secondary line, title at 5 percent of canvas height and caption at 1.5 percent, left-aligned to the left edge of the upper-right negative space and baseline-aligned to the top third. Keep tracking slightly open, color #E2D3B8 at 85 percent opacity, and never let type cross the mugs or the brightest linen fold.

Grading. The single most important tone decision is to warm the shadows toward umber while keeping highlights neutral cream, because that split preserves the cool teal glaze as an accent, gives the linen its tactile warmth, and creates the moody depth without turning the image muddy or sepia.
